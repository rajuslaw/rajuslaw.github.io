AI-Powered Invoice Extraction

An OCR + LLM pipeline that auto-populates invoice fields in an internal invoicing application, replacing slow, error-prone manual data entry.

Role Technical Lead / Principal
Domain Finance Operations
Pattern OCR + LLM Extraction
Control Human Approval

01 — Problem

Manual invoice entry was creating avoidable downstream work.

Around 30% of monthly payment-request-form (PRF) rejections came from data mismatches. Those rejections created payment delays and vendor escalations.

The goal was not simply to extract text from invoices. The system needed to identify the right business fields across invoices that differed significantly in layout, wording, and structure.

Core engineering problem

Build an automation layer that improves data-entry speed and accuracy without allowing an AI extraction error to become an incorrect payment record.

02 — Why AI

OCR reads the document. LLM extraction interprets it.

Vendors use different invoice formats and inconsistent field labels. Traditional OCR can extract the text, but text extraction alone does not reliably determine which value belongs to which business field.

Traditional approach

OCR + rules

  • Extract text from the document
  • Identify fields using fixed patterns or vendor rules
  • Requires rules to evolve as formats change
Chosen approach

OCR + LLM

  • OCR reads the document pixels
  • LLM interprets field meaning and context
  • Generalizes across varied vendor formats

The LLM is used for interpretation and structured extraction; it is not the final authority for payment approval.

03 — Architecture

Two entry points, one controlled extraction pipeline.

Phase 1 — Inline auto-fill
01 User uploads invoice PDF / image
→
02 Extraction service OCR + LLM
→
03 Structured fields Invoice data
→
04 Human verifies Before submission
Phase 2 — Email-based draft PRF
01 Shared mailbox Invoice attachment
→
02 Background job Extract attachment
→
03 Extraction service OCR + LLM
→
04 Draft PRF + review Team approval
Design principle: AI accelerates the workflow; humans retain approval of the business record.

04 — Human-in-the-Loop

Automation without surrendering business control.

Both rollout phases keep a human in the loop. In the inline flow, the extracted fields are populated into the existing PRF form for verification. In the email flow, a draft PRF is created and routed to the invoicing team for review.

07 — Trade-offs

Where the design deliberately chose flexibility and control.

01

OCR + LLM instead of regex-heavy extraction

Vendor invoices vary significantly. Context-based extraction was selected to generalize across formats rather than creating a growing collection of vendor-specific rules.

02

Human review in both phases

Invoice errors can have financial consequences, so automation was intentionally designed as an accelerator rather than an autonomous approver.

03

Two entry points

The solution supports both user-uploaded invoices and invoices arriving through the shared mailbox.

04

Structured fields inside the existing application

The extraction output is integrated into the existing PRF experience, avoiding a separate workflow or user retraining.

08 — Design FAQ

Questions I would expect in an architecture review.

Why not create a template for every vendor?

Per-vendor templates do not scale well when invoice formats change or the number of vendors grows. The LLM-based approach uses context to generalize across different formats.

Why keep a human in the loop?

The workflow deals with invoice information that can affect payments. Human verification provides a control point before the extracted data becomes part of the business workflow.

How is accuracy evaluated?

The success framework compares auto-fill accuracy, PRF creation time, and rejection rate against the manual baseline.

What is the role of the LLM?

The LLM interprets the OCR output and extracts the required structured fields. It is not responsible for final payment approval.
